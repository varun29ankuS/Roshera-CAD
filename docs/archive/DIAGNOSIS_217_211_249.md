### 217-A. Void-shell cone 361 falls back to an untrimmed grid; 362 and 363 only inherit it

**Root cause.** `tessellate_cone_apex_cap` (`geometry-engine/src/tessellation/surface.rs:4174`)
refuses the face at its on-cone test (line 4248): `tol = max(1e-6, 1e-6 * scale)`, about
2.6e-6 here, but two rim edges are tolerant intersection curves 1e-5 off the cone. The face
then goes to `tessellate_curved_cdt`, which fails because the seam ruling to the apex is
walked out and back at the same u, and the code falls to the UNTRIMMED grid.

**Evidence (measured).**
- `ROSHERA_TESS_TRACE=1 tess_one_face.exe $P/217/output.step 361,362,363`:
  `[curved-cdt] face 361 kind=Cone FAILED OutlineOverlapsItself { segments: 1 }` then
  `[tess] FALLBACK cone face 361: ... -> UNTRIMMED grid`. Faces 362 and 363 mesh by
  curved-CDT with no failure.
- `PROBE_FACE=361`: loop = 878 (NURBS), 877 rev, 877 fwd (the seam, v 0.792 -> 0 -> 0.792),
  876 (NURBS), 875 (Arc). The rim's u runs 4.402 -> 2pi -> 0 -> 2.337 -> 4.402: once round
  the axis, monotone, so the apex fan is valid for it.
- `PROBE_EDGEGAP=876`: `worst distance to each face [1.06e-5, 8.2e-4]` (cone first).
  `PROBE_EDGEGAP=878`: `[8.64e-6, 8.2e-4]`. Arc 875: 6.7e-12.
- Open-edge arithmetic: 362 has 191 boundary points = edge 879 (100) + arc 875 (91), open 91.
  363 has 216 = 879 (100) + 876 (58) + 878 (58), open 116. Each face's open count is exactly
  its border with 361. All are "no neighbour point".

**Confidence.** High that 361's fallback is the single cause of all 307 open edges. High on
the tolerance refusal by arithmetic (1.06e-5 > 2.6e-6); the branch itself was not traced.

**Fix.** In `tessellate_cone_apex_cap`, accept rim samples within the edge's own measured gap
to the cone instead of 1e-6 (the rim is used verbatim, so facet error is bounded by that
gap). Keep the refusals that matter: one nappe, no sample at the apex, winding of 2pi, and
add a u-monotone (star-shaped) check, which the function does not have today. Optional
second layer: in `curved_cdt::project_loop_to_uv`, spread a cone's apex sample over the
u-interval between its two seam traversals so the outline does not double back.

**RED test.** A cone to its apex, loop = seam ruling twice + a rim made of an arc and two
NURBS curves displaced 1e-5 normal to the cone. Assert every boundary sample of the face is a
mesh vertex and no mesh edge is open against a neighbour sharing the rim. Fails today
(untrimmed grid). Mutation: restore the 1e-6 gate.

### 217-B. Closed NURBS tube 81: the ring stitch pairs samples by index, not by position

**Root cause.** `tessellate_nurbs_skin_lateral` (`surface.rs:7057`) was written for
kernel-built lofts. It assumes both rim edges are sampled in +u starting at u = 0, uniformly.
`stitch_closed_rings` (`surface.rs:9183`) then walks the two rows by index fraction, and
`emit_outward_triangle` flips each triangle on its own, which hides the damage from the
normal check. On this imported face the top rim (edge 179) runs opposite to the grid, the
seam sits at u = 0.018, and the bottom rim (edge 200) is sampled non-uniformly.

**Evidence (measured).** `PROBE_DUMP=1 tess_one_face.exe $P/217/output.step 81`, then
`python -I mesh_dump_stats.py <dump> 100` (17 rows x 100 vertices):
- `row 15->16: aligned-as-is cost 170.786; best cost 2.746 at shift 97 reversed True`.
- Triangles with an edge over 2 mm, per band: bands 0-14 have 0; band 15 has **184 of 200**,
  longest edge 13.25 mm (the tube is about 13.7 mm across). Those triangles cut through the
  tube.
- `same-way directed edges: 149` inside the face, the probe's `sameway ... 81 / 81: 149`.
- Row 0 (edge 200): segment lengths 0.022 to 1.52 mm against 0.42 uniform on row 1.
- `PROBE_SELFX=1`: all three crossing pairs involve face 81: (65,81), (81,81), (81,124).

This one cause accounts for 81's same-way 160 (149 + 11 with face 88), both
boundary-crossing facets, the 83.6 deg deviation, the off-surface facet, and the part's
`self_intersection_free=false`.

**Confidence.** High for band 15. Medium that band 0's skew alone gives the 11 same-way edges
with 88.

**Fix.** One helper, "stitch two closed rings by a shared monotone parameter": give each ring
sample its u (surface `closest_point`, unwrapped), reverse a ring that runs backwards, rotate
to a common start, then advance whichever ring's next sample has the smaller u. Wind the
whole band by one decision, not per triangle. If a rim is not monotone in u, decline to the
unrolled CDT (`tessellate_nurbs_skin_lateral_cap_notch`). The same helper fixes 249-A.

**RED test.** A closed-u NURBS tube whose top rim edge is stored reversed, seam at u = 0.02,
bottom rim sampled non-uniformly. Assert no triangle edge longer than 3 rim segments, zero
same-way edges, mesh area within 1% of the surface area. Mutation: restore index-fraction
advance.

**Secondary, not verified.** Faces 81 and 88 keep 1 + 3 open edges at the rim/seam corner.
`edge_cache.rs` snaps open edges to their vertices but leaves a closed edge untouched;
`PROBE_FACE` shows edge 200's curve start 1.27e-3 from its vertex while seam edges 201 / 241
are snapped to it. Re-probe after 217-B before acting.

### 211-A. "Planar face 415 self-overlaps" is false: the validator does not snap edge ends

**Root cause.** `planar_face_self_intersects` (`operations/geometry_validity.rs:88`) samples
each edge from its own curve over [0, 1). At a tolerant vertex one curve ends up to 1e-3
from where the next begins, so the polygon gets a tiny jump segment and the two curves cross
beside it. The mesher's `EdgeSampleCache` snaps ends to the vertex, which is why the mesh is
fine.

**Evidence (measured).**
- `OCCT_FACE=415 occt_planar_loop_crossing.py`: 31 edges, end-to-next-start jumps up to
  1.058e-3, edge tolerances to 3.1e-2.
  `curve ends as evaluated (validator's way): polygon 5039 points, proper crossings 2`
  (edges 25/26 and 6/7, index gap 2).
  `edge ends snapped to one shared point: polygon 5039 points, proper crossings 0`.
- Roshera's own mesh polygon: `PROBE_DUMP=1 tess_one_face ... 415` then
  `polygon_crossings.py`: 1783 vertices, `proper crossings: 0`.

**Confidence.** High on the mechanism. The re-enactment uses OCCT's curves, not Roshera's,
so the exact crossing pair in Roshera is not shown.

**Fix.** Build the validator's polygon from the same snapped samples the mesher uses: start
each edge at its walk-start vertex position, not `evaluate(0)`. Same family as the
sagitta fix for parts 131 / 134 / 249.

**RED test.** Planar face, two arcs meeting at a shallow angle, the first curve overshooting
the shared vertex by 5e-4. Assert `planar_face_self_intersects == false`; keep the true
bow-tie test red. Mutation: revert to `evaluate(0)`.

### 211-B. Twelve extrusion faces are wound backwards (4,000+ same-way edges)

**Root cause.** For a non-`Plane` surface, `tessellate_planar_face` (`surface.rs:831-849`)
takes the outward normal as `Newell(loop) * face.orientation.sign()`. That assumes the loop
winds counter-clockwise about the SURFACE normal. A STEP face with `same_sense = .F.` winds
about the FACE normal, so the sign is applied twice. True `Plane` faces escape because the
code aligns Newell to the analytic normal first.

**Evidence (measured).** `ROSHERA_TESS_TRACE=1 tess_one_face.exe` on all 56 ruled faces,
crossed with the certificate's `blocking: ... tessellation face ... "Ruled"` lines:

| path | sense | verdict | faces |
|---|---|---|---|
| planar path | Backward | flipped | 10 |
| planar path | Backward | ok | 0 |
| planar path | Forward | ok | 6 |
| curved-CDT | Forward / Backward | ok | 20 / 18 |
| curved-CDT at display chord, planar at certificate chord | Backward | flipped | 2 (201, 403) |

Faces 201 and 403 report 299 off-surface facets = 301 boundary points - 2: a boundary-only
triangulation, the planar path at the certificate's coarser chord. Each flipped face shows
`normal_agreement 1.0, analytic_normal_agreement 0.0`. `PROBE_SAMEWAY=1` lists 70 face
pairs; every pair but one (437/438) includes one of these twelve.

**Second defect found on the way (measured).** Face 32 is not flat: by hand from the OCCT
vertices, its fourth corner is 0.54 mm off the plane of the other three. It reaches the
planar path through `surface.is_planar(Tolerance::new(chord, max_angle_deviation))`
(`primitives/surface.rs:863`), which accepts any surface whose normals stay within the
ANGLE tolerance (0.1 rad). The face is then triangulated from its boundary alone. The census
counts 20 such faces in 211. This breaks the chord tolerance without tripping the
certificate, since the 6 Forward ones pass.

**Confidence.** High on the winding cause (10/10 and 6/6 split, plus 2 explained). High that
face 32 is non-flat; the 0.1 rad figure is read from code.

**Fix.** (1) In `tessellate_planar_face`, for every surface type, align Newell to the
surface's analytic normal at a footpoint ON the face (a vote over a few boundary samples),
then apply `orientation.sign()`. The loop's stored direction stops mattering. (2) Send a
non-`Plane` face to the planar path only when its boundary samples fit a plane within the
chord tolerance (`is_face_loop_planar_in_3d`), not on the angular `is_planar`.

**RED test.** Import a STEP `SURFACE_OF_LINEAR_EXTRUSION` face with `.F.` sense on a
straight-line profile inside a closed box. Assert zero same-way edges and
`analytic_normal_agreement == 1.0`. Second test: a gently curved extrusion (normals within
0.05 rad, 0.5 mm bow) must have interior vertices and chord error within tolerance. Run the
full planar-face suite afterwards: kernel-built ruled faces rely on today's rule.

### 211-C. A B-spline edge keeps its whole curve when its vertex is over 1e-3 off it

**Root cause.** `span_between_vertices` (`export-engine/src/formats/step/handlers/tier1/topology.rs:654`)
returns `Err` when a vertex is farther than `VERTEX_ON_CURVE = 1e-3` from the curve, and the
caller then keeps the WHOLE curve. The constant's comment says writers' vertex tolerances
are well inside 1e-3. They are not: OCCT stores vertex tolerances up to 3.6e-2 on this part.

**Evidence (measured).**
- STEP: `#40167 = EDGE_CURVE('',#40141,#40168,#40170,.T.)`. Its 3D curve `#40171` is a cubic
  Bezier from (-66.952, 11.305, 85.002) to (-66.900, 13.554, 84.993), but the end vertex
  `#40168` is at (-66.926, 12.447, 85.063), the middle. Its pcurve's knots stop at 0.50796.
- `occt_vertex_on_curve.py` on 211:
  `off 1.543e-03 vertex-tol 3.0e-02 ... len 1.1446 uses 0.508 of curve; faces [120, 225]` and
  `off 1.543e-03 ... len 1.1099 uses 0.492 of curve; faces [120, 225]`.
- `PROBE_FACE=NurbsSurface`, face 225: edges 409 and 407 both run u 0 -> 1 along v = 0, with
  `start gap 1.14e0` and `end gap 1.11e0`. The same border is covered twice.
- Trace: `[curved-cdt] face 225 kind=NurbsSurface FAILED OutlineOverlapsItself { segments: 2 }`,
  `face 225 ...: 0 tris`.
- Consequences match by count: torus 224 open 31 and 226 open 32 are their borders with 225
  (edges of length 3.05 and 3.12); NURBS 348 open 23 is its border with 225; sphere 120
  carries the same two edges.

**Confidence.** High.

**Fix.** When the closest-point parameter is interior, trim there and record the distance in
the warning; never widen to the whole curve. Bound "broken data" by something scaled to the
edge (for example a fraction of the shorter piece's length), and return a typed failure for
that edge beyond it.

**RED test.** Two `EDGE_CURVE`s sharing one Bezier, split at a vertex placed 1.5e-3 off the
curve. Assert the ranges are [0, ~0.5] and [~0.5, 1] and the face meshes with no open edge.
Mutation: restore the whole-curve fallback.

### 211-D. Sphere faces that straddle the seam get the non-conforming grid

**Root cause.** `tessellate_spherical_face` (`surface.rs:3512`) tries five special paths and
otherwise uses a membership-gated grid (`tessellate_spherical_with_poles`), which keeps grid
points inside the trim and never meets the boundary samples. There is no edge-driven
fallback like the torus has (`face_has_a_trim_edge` -> curved-CDT).

**Evidence (measured).** A natural control: faces 95 and 110 are mirror twins (same radius,
same three bounding circles, pole on the boundary).
- `occt_face.py`: face 95 `faceUV (0.0, 6.28319, ...)`, wire carries the seam meridian twice
  plus a degenerate pole edge. Face 110 `faceUV (1.55285, 4.73033, ...)`, no seam.
- Certificate: 95 `open_edges 102`, all "no neighbour point"; 110 has no open edge.
- Same pairing for 120 (seam, open 50; also hit by 211-C) and 83 (no seam, watertight).
- `tess_one_face`: 95 gives 55499 triangles in 3 ms, a plain grid.

**Confidence.** High that the seam is the discriminator. Which special path accepts 110 and
declines 95 was not traced.

**Fix.** Before the special paths, strip seam edges (walked twice) and degenerate pole edges
from the loop, as commit 135d55bc did for the cone. If all paths still decline and the face
has a trim edge, mesh it from its edges (pole fan along meridians to the cached rim, or
curved-CDT), and emit nothing rather than a non-conforming grid.

**RED test.** Import both a seam-straddling polar sphere face and its mirror. Assert both
are watertight against their neighbours. Mutation: remove the seam strip.

### 211-E. Nine spheres flagged "boundary-crossing" for one tiny facet beside the pole (false)

**Root cause.** OCCT writes a micro circle round the pole (length 0.004) instead of a
degenerate edge. A facet with a vertex on it has longitudes spanning half a turn, and
`at_pole_point` (`harness/watertight.rs:1024`) only excuses vertices within `size * 1e-6`
(about 1.4e-5) of the pole.

**Evidence (measured).** `sphere_polar_facets.py` on the dumps of faces 110 and 83: exactly
one facet each, `u-coverage 3.1416 rad; longest 3D edge 1.194e-01; vertex distances to the
pole 1.29e-03 ...` (83: 3.1494 rad, 1.075e-01, 7.11e-04). The certificate reports
`boundary_crossing_facets 1` with a normal deviation of 1-3 deg on 110, 467, 589, 364, 604,
445, 68, 455, 83. Face 455 did not reproduce with OCCT's axis, so it is unconfirmed.

**Confidence.** High for 110 and 83; the other seven by pattern.

**Fix.** Treat a facet as at a singularity when a vertex lies within the facet's own longest
edge of a pole. A real bridge has edges far longer than its distance to any pole.

**RED test.** A sphere cap whose pole is ringed by a 6e-4 radius circle edge. Assert
`boundary_crossing_facets == 0`. Keep the 86 mm bore-bridge test red.

### 249-A. Plane 48: the annulus strip is stitched by index fraction

**Root cause.** `annulus_radial_strip` (`surface.rs:1504`) aligns only the first inner point
to the first outer point by angle, then advances by `i/n <= j/m`. That is right only if both
rings are sampled uniformly in angle. Per-triangle flipping hides the folds. Same defect as
217-B.

**Evidence (measured).**
- `PROBE_FACE=48`: outer loop of 24 edges with sample counts
  `[11, 4, 6, 7, 17, 101, 17, 7, 6, 4, 11, 2, ...]`; the hole is one circle, 101 samples.
- `PROBE_DUMP=1 tess_one_face ... 48`, analysed: 462 triangles,
  `unsigned area 1927.5135` against OCCT's face area 1893.9329 (33.6 mm2 of overlap),
  `same-way directed edges inside the face: 24`.
- Example spoke: outer point at 22.2 deg joined to an inner point at 46.8 deg, across an
  annulus 5 mm wide.
- `PROBE_SAMEWAY=1`: `48 / 48: 20` and `48 / 102: 10`. Torus 102's same-way edges are its
  shared circle with 48.

**Confidence.** High.

**Fix.** The shared ring-stitch helper from 217-B, keyed on angle about the common centre.
Also tighten the "circular" gate: this outer ring has two flats and still passed the 2% test.

**RED test.** Annulus whose outer ring is an arc with 101 samples plus short fillets and a
2-sample flat, inner ring a uniform circle. Assert mesh area equals the annulus area within
1e-6 relative and zero same-way edges. Mutation: restore the index-fraction advance.

### 249-B. Revolution 199: `closest_point` assumes the profile lies in one meridian plane

**Root cause.** `SurfaceOfRevolution::closest_point` (`primitives/surface.rs:5904`) measures
the angle v from the profile's direction at u = 0 and finds u by rotating the query onto
that one meridian. Here the profile circle's azimuth about the axis changes along it, so
every boundary sample is placed at the wrong v. Curved-CDT then joins true boundary points
to interior points evaluated at shifted parameters, and the mesh folds.

**Evidence (measured).**
- `occt_revolution_profile.py` on 249: `face #199 ... basis Circle, axis (0,0,-1) at
  (20.6341, -30.7612, 1.9811); profile azimuth spread 0.018709 rad over the face's range;
  ... miss up to 2.4423 mm`. It is the only revolution face on the part.
- `PROBE_EDGEGAP=456` (an arc 5e-12 from cylinder 129, and 1e-12 from face 199 per OCCT):
  `worst distance to each face [5.06e-12, 3.6098]`.
- `PROBE_TESSFACE=199`: `closest_point residual worst 3.610e0`, facets 179 deg off.
- Trace: `[turned] SurfaceOfRevolution 275 of 64975 facet(s) still turned`.
- `PROBE_SELFX=1`: three of the five crossing pairs involve 199: (192,199), (199,199),
  (199,220).

**Confidence.** High.

**Fix.** Find u by matching (height, radius) against the profile's own (h(u), r(u)), then
set v = azimuth(point) - azimuth(P(u)). For a meridian profile this reduces to today's
result.

**RED test.** Revolve a circle whose plane is offset from the axis. For 200 points
`S(u, v)`, assert `|S(closest_point(p)) - p| < 1e-9`. Fails today by millimetres.

### 249-C. Thin fillet tori 223 / 70 (and 161): needle triangles in the native chart

**Root cause.** These are fillets with minor radius 0.5 on a major radius of about 205: 188
mm long, 0.785 mm wide. `ScaledChart::for_face` (`curved_cdt.rs:1286`) exists for exactly
this, but returns `None` for any periodic surface other than NURBS or revolution, so a torus
is meshed in raw (u, v), where a step in u is about 410 times longer than a step in v.
Delaunay there joins boundary points to interior points several samples away along u; the
arc's sagitta over that span is as large as the point's inset, so the facet turns over.

**Evidence (measured).**
- `occt_face.py`: 223 `MajorRadius=205.337 MinorRadius=0.5`, faceUV u span 0.916, v span
  1.5708; five edges (one long side is split in two, so the quad-patch path declines).
- Trace: `[turned] Torus 69 of 16095 facet(s) still turned` (223), `39 of 27859` (70).
- `PROBE_TESSFACE=223`: 269 turned at the certificate chord; the examples are all two
  boundary points on z = -0.01894 plus one point 9e-4 above, 4.7 mm further along the arc,
  179.9 deg off. Arithmetic: sagitta over 6.6 mm at R = 205 is 0.0265 mm; the point's inset
  is 0.03 mm.
- Certificate: `worst aspect ratio 5953.6 ... (worst face 70)`.

**Confidence.** Medium-high. The exclusion is read from code; the needles are measured.

**Fix.** Allow `ScaledChart` for a torus PATCH whose unwrapped boundary stays inside one
period in both directions (no seam involved). The code comment records that rescaling tori
broke part 146, so gate on "does not reach the seam" and re-run 146. Alternative: let
`tessellate_torus_quad_patch` merge consecutive edges on one iso-line into a side, and
stitch rows with the helper from 217-B.

**RED test.** Torus patch R = 200, r = 0.5, quarter turn in v, 0.9 rad in u, one long side
split into two edges. Assert zero turned facets and max normal deviation under the angle
tolerance. Mutation: restore the exclusion.
